# Risk register — Fourth Down Engine

Each row keeps its history. A superseded mitigation is struck through, not deleted.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|---|------|-----------|--------|------------|--------|
| 1 | **Selection on true distance.** Play-by-play records yards to go as a whole number. Teams that go on "4th & 1" are closer to the line than teams that kick (0.70 vs 0.98 yd; Lopez 2020). The conversion model overstates success for kicking teams, so it overstates the value of going on short yardage. | High | Medium | Conversion is fit on 3rd and 4th downs pooled with a 4th-down term, as Romer (2006) and Brill et al. (2025) do. Coaches are charged only for confident mistakes. The white paper states the direction of the bias. | Accepted, documented. 1.1.0: the grader judges 4th & 1 at 0.70 yd (went) and 0.98 yd (kicked), and the app asks inches or a full yard. |
| 2 | **Selection on long-yardage attempts.** 4th & 4+ attempts happen mostly in desperate late spots against soft defenses. Hold-out conversion on 4th & 11+ is 0.33 actual vs 0.22 predicted. | High | Low | The model is not fit to the selected 4th-down rate, so it is if anything conservative about going on long yardage. Documented in the white paper. | Accepted, documented |
| 3 | **Point estimates presented as precise.** Many 4th-down edges are smaller than the model's own uncertainty (Brill, Yurko & Wyner 2025). | High | High | Whole-pipeline bootstrap (20 game-resampled refits). The app shows how many replicates agree. The coach grader charges only calls where at least 90% of replicates agree. Edges under 1 point are labelled toss-ups. | Mitigated |
| 4 | **Bootstrap resamples games only, not drives within games.** It may understate variance. Brill et al. resample both. | Medium | Low | Documented in the white paper (section 6). Twenty replicates bound the confidence share in steps of 5%. | Accepted, documented |
| 5 | **Model fit and grading share seasons.** Coaches' 2014–2025 calls are graded by a model fit on those seasons. | Medium | Medium | Option values were validated out of sample on 2024–2025 (calibration within 1 point for go, field goal and punt). 2026 calls are fully out of sample. | Accepted, documented |
| 6 | **Rule changes.** Kickoff touchbacks moved to the 30 (2024) and the 35 (2025). The kickoff spot changes the value of every score. | Certain (recurring) | Medium | The kickoff spot is measured from `rules_season` in `engine/config.py`; one edit per rule change. | Mitigated |
| 7 | **Field-goal curve extrapolation.** The distance² term bends upward past ~83 yd; an early build credited 87-yd kicks with real odds. | Was live in development | High | Fixed before release: kicks over 70 yd (the longest attempt in the data) are out of range. A test checks the curve falls at every yard from 18 to 70. | Resolved |
| 8 | **Validation leakage of the rule era.** An early build measured the kickoff spot from a held-out season, got NaN, and placed kickoffs at the 1-yard line. Field-goal option values read 0.44 vs 0.56 actual. | Was live in development | High | Fixed before release: the kickoff spot is a rule, measured from full data; an assertion fails the fit on NaN. | Resolved |
| 9 | **Python and JavaScript engines drift.** The app runs `app/engine.js`; grading and docs use `engine/decide.py`. | Medium | High | CI runs a parity test on 60 random states with all toggle mixes; tolerance 1e-9. | Mitigated |
| 10 | **Weather data gaps.** The structured wind/temperature fields are missing for 46% of outdoor games in 2022. | Certain | Low | The free-text weather string fills them. Remaining gaps are imputed with the outdoor median, and the count is logged. Weather is a toggle, off by default. | Mitigated |
| 11 | **Coach name errors in the source.** nflverse spells one coach "Klint Kubliak". | Low | Low | `coach_aliases` in `engine/config.py` corrects the display name. | Mitigated |
| 12 | **Kicker skill estimates are small-sample.** Kicker effects improve hold-out log loss only slightly (0.3673 to 0.366). | High | Low | Shrinkage toward the league with prior widths chosen out of sample. Fifty-percent distances above 70 yd are reported as "70+" rather than extrapolated. | Accepted, documented |
| 15 | **Unofficial live feed.** Live game mode reads ESPN's public scoreboard (site.web.api.espn.com; site.api.espn.com is blocked for browsers), which has no published contract and can change, lag or fail. | Medium | Low | Every field is optional; missing fields leave the form unchanged; the user checks the form before the call; the private Claude copy hides the feature. The in-game fields (down, distance, spot, timeouts) are tested on a fixture built from the feed's field names and still need checking against a live game. | Accepted, documented |
| 16 | **Past seasons graded under today's kickoff rule.** Fixed in 1.2.0; see the changelog. | Was live (1.0.0–1.1.0) | Medium | Each graded decision now carries its season's measured kickoff spot; a hand-derived test checks a 2015 field goal uses the 2015 spot. | Resolved |
| 13 | **Raw data redistribution.** nflverse data has its own terms. | Low | Medium | Raw files are not committed (`.gitignore`). Only derived model parameters ship. See ADR-0001. | Mitigated |
| 14 | **Cost totals exceed published estimates.** League confident-mistake cost per team-season is larger than every published estimate of fourth-down conservatism (0.22–0.4 wins). Toss-up share, confident share and clear-go share match the literature; the size of the edges does not. | Certain (measured) | High | Grades are percentile ranks, not absolute counts. The white paper (section 7) and the app's Coaches tab say so. Candidate fixes for the next version: a joint yards-gained model (nfl4th), a distance-precision adjustment (Lopez 2020), and drive-level bootstrap. | Accepted, documented — fix owed. 1.1.0: overtime, touchdown tries and the 4th & 1 correction moved the totals little (2025: 0.61 to 0.62). Edge check: goers -0.6 ± 0.6 WP points vs prediction relative to kickers; -7.4 ± 3.2 where the engine favors going by 8+ points, so the overstatement sits in extreme, mostly late-game spots. |
